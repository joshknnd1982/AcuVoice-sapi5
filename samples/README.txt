AcuVoice SAPI5 -- sample wave files
==================================

All of these were generated by AcuVoiceDiagnostics.exe, not picked by hand. They are
16-bit mono PCM at 16000 Hz, which is what the wrapper hands SAPI. The engine itself
produces 8000 Hz 8-bit mu-law; everything above that is the wrapper's doing.

report.txt is the diagnostics run that produced them: the engine's own report, the
parameter ranges, the voice list, and the latency figures.


engine\  --  the engine driven directly, with no SAPI in the path
-----------------------------------------------------------------
01_default                 the engine exactly as AcuVoice shipped it

02_speed_085..350_wpm      the rate scale end to end. 175 wpm is untouched engine audio;
                           85 doubles the duration, 350 halves it. avcore parses \spd=
                           but never acts on it, so this is the wrapper's WSOLA time
                           scaling following avcore's own words-per-minute curve.

03_pitch_45..91            the pitch scale end to end, which is six semitones either way
                           around 63. The durations are all the same, which is the point:
                           the pitch moves and the timing does not.

04_volume_06553..65535     the volume scale. 32767 is the recorded level.

05_pauses_default/none/long
                           the same three sentences with the engine's own pause lengths
                           (6.2 s), with them set to zero (3.9 s), and with them long
                           (9.3 s). These are real engine pauses, not silence pasted in:
                           the pause lengths are the one prosody control avcore applies
                           itself.

06_voice_1..7              each of the seven published voices saying what it is.

07_numbers                 "In 1998 the price rose 7 percent to $42.50, and 3.14159 is
                           close enough to pi. Call 555-1234 at 9:30 a.m. on July 4th."
                           None of that expansion is the wrapper's work -- AcuVoice has
                           its own English front end and it is good.
08_abbreviations           Dr., N., St., Mon., Fri., approx., hrs.
09_punctuation             dashes, question and exclamation marks, brackets, ellipsis
10_symbols                 an email address, a URL, a percentage
11_accents                 cafe, Zurich, naive, resume, jalapenos, curly quotes and an
                           em dash -- everything Windows-1252 cannot hold, transliterated
12_long_paragraph          five sentences of running text
13_spelled                 what SPVA_SpellOut produces
14_punctuation_named       "C:\Program Files\AcuVoice (x86)" with punctuation spoken

15_tags_bookmark           the engine's own tag parser turned on: \mrk=42\ reaches
                           get_bookmark and the tag itself is not spoken
16_tags_literal            the same parser turned off, so a Windows path containing
                           \spd=250\ is read as a path

17_fast_and_low            the fastest and lowest the wrapper will go
18_slow_and_high           the slowest and highest


sapi\  --  the same engine through the real SAPI 5 interface
-------------------------------------------------------------
sapi_AcuVoice_*            each of the eight registered voices, spoken by
                           ISpVoice::Speak into a wave file. These are the ones that
                           prove the wrapper rather than the engine: SAPI created the
                           voice from its registry token, called GetOutputFormat, and
                           drove Speak.

sapi_host_rate_minus10     the host's own rate control at -10 (quarter speed)
sapi_host_rate_plus10      and at +10 (four times), on top of the voice's own setting
sapi_host_volume_25/100    the host's own volume control

The 32-bit in-process path and the 64-bit worker path were both run over this set. Nine
of the twelve files come out byte-identical; the other three differ by exactly one sample
out of a quarter of a million, where x87 and SSE2 rounding split a WSOLA similarity tie
differently.
